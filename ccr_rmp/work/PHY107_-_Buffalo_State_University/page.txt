🎯 Course Description
This course is designed to introduce the principles of Newtonian Mechanics. Students in science and engineering majors take this course in their first year to develop foundations on key concepts including scalar quantities, measurement, equations of motion, Newton's Laws, laws of conservation, linear and angular momentum, gravitation, kinetic theory, and thermodynamics.
🧩 Topics Covered
Introduction to Physics
– Measurement, base units, scientific methodology
Vectors and Scalars
– Addition and product rules, vector operations
One-Dimensional Motion
– Displacement, velocity, acceleration, free fall
Two and Three-Dimensional Motion
– Projectile motion, 3D kinematics
Newton's Laws of Motion
– First, second, and third laws, applications
Friction and Forces
– Types of friction, force analysis
Work and Energy
– Work-energy theorem, work done by special forces
Conservation Laws
– Potential energy, conservation of energy and momentum
Center of Mass and Linear Momentum
– Momentum conservation, collisions
Rotational Motion
– Moment of inertia, kinetic energy of rotation, torque
Angular Momentum
– Conservation of angular momentum, parallel axis theorem
Equilibrium and Elasticity
– Static equilibrium, material properties
Gravitation
– Superposition principle, gravitational forces
Orbital Mechanics
– Potential energy, Kepler's laws, satellites and orbits
Simple Harmonic Motion
– Mass-spring systems, energy in SHM
Wave Mechanics
– Wave equation, standing and traveling waves
Thermodynamics
– Temperature, heat, thermal variables and systems
First Law of Thermodynamics
– Energy conservation in thermal systems
Kinetic Theory of Gases
– Molecular theory, gas laws, kinetic energy
Second Law of Thermodynamics
– Entropy, thermodynamic processes
🎓 Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
LO1:
Analyze scientific problems using mathematical tools, particularly vectors
LO2:
Interpret the physical meaning of conservation laws in mechanical systems
LO3:
Apply calculus concepts to understand and solve problems in Newtonian mechanics
LO4:
Understand the fundamental ideas behind wave mechanics and thermodynamics
LO5:
Solve complex physics problems using multiple conservation principles
LO6:
Apply physics concepts to real-world engineering and scientific applications
